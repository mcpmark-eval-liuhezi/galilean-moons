# The Galilean Moons of Jupiter

The four largest moons of Jupiter — **Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto** — are known as the Galilean moons. Listed below in order of distance from Jupiter, innermost first.

They are named after **Galileo Galilei**, who observed them with his telescope in January 1610 and recognized them as satellites of Jupiter in March 1610. They were the first Solar System objects discovered since antiquity and the first objects found to orbit a planet other than Earth. Simon Marius independently observed the moons on 8 January 1610 and gave them their present names, after mythological companions of Zeus.

The three inner moons — Io, Europa, and Ganymede — are locked in a 4:2:1 orbital resonance with each other.

## Io (innermost)

- **Diameter:** 3,643 km
- **Orbital period:** 1.77 days (~42.5 hours)
- **Standout trait:** The most volcanically active body in the Solar System, with over 400 active volcanoes driven by tidal heating.

## Europa

- **Diameter:** 3,122 km
- **Orbital period:** 3.55 days
- **Standout trait:** An icy moon with a smooth, geologically young surface hiding a probable global subsurface saltwater ocean.

## Ganymede

- **Diameter:** 5,268 km
- **Orbital period:** 7.15 days
- **Standout trait:** The largest moon in the Solar System (bigger than the planet Mercury) and the only moon known to generate its own magnetic field.

## Callisto (outermost)

- **Diameter:** 4,821 km
- **Orbital period:** 16.69 days
- **Standout trait:** Has the oldest and most heavily cratered surface in the Solar System, essentially unchanged for ~4 billion years.
